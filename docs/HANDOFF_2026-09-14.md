## 1. What the paper is

**An audit, not a model paper.** The question: *do published interpretability claims in
drug–target interaction prediction survive realistic evaluation?* Attention-based DTI models
claim their attention marks binding sites. We test that across four split levels — random,
cold-drug, cold-target, cold-pair — on DAVIS, with KIBA as replication.

Two axes, each against its own control:
* **plausibility** — precision@10 of the explanation against a ground truth, beside chance
  and the achievable ceiling;
* **faithfulness** — change in prediction when the attended residues are masked, minus the
  same for a random-masking control.

**Subjects:** HyperAttentionDTI (published), MolTrans (published), ColdSite-DTI (ours, held
to the same standard), DeepDTA (accuracy anchor only — no attention, never audited).

## 2. The findings, with the numbers

### The audit table (DAVIS, precision@10 vs UniProt annotated residues)

One Holm–Bonferroni correction over **all sixteen cells** (3 audited models + uniform
control × 4 levels). Source: `results/analysis_davis_policyA/audit_davis_binary.md`.

| model | random | cold-drug | cold-target | cold-pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ColdSite-DTI | 0.015 ± 0.007 | 0.022 ± 0.009 | 0.017 ± 0.002 | 0.013 ± 0.005 |
| HyperAttentionDTI | **0.034 ± 0.006** | 0.040 ± 0.031 | 0.024 ± 0.008 | 0.022 ± 0.010 |
| MolTrans | 0.021 ± 0.003 | 0.027 ± 0.005 | 0.028 ± 0.016 | 0.020 ± 0.014 |
| uniform control | 0.020 ± 0.001 | 0.020 ± 0.001 | 0.018 ± 0.005 | 0.017 ± 0.001 |

**One of sixteen survives: HyperAttentionDTI at random**, p = 0.001996 against a threshold
of 0.003125, 1.7× chance. It beats a borrowed-map null and an amino-acid-preserving
permutation in every seed (both 0.021, p = 0.001), and a within-span permutation in two
seeds of three. Read against the positive control's dose curve it is worth an **equivalent
dose of 0.007–0.017** — about 1% of the annotated sites ranked first. Real, reproducible,
and small.

MolTrans sits at the metric's floor in all four cells. ColdSite-DTI is at chance at all
four, and at random its whole bootstrap interval (0.015 [0.013–0.018]) lies *below* the
0.020 chance level.

### The three findings that may outlast the verdict

1. **Readout dependence (§7b).** Between the tensor inside a network and one weight per
   residue, somebody chooses an axis, a layer, a projection. Across 25 proteins, an
   alternative readout's top-ten residues overlap the published readout's by **2–12%** (one
   exception at 90%). HyperAttentionDTI's KLIFS agreement at cold-target reads **0.367**
   under channel-max and **0.081** — below the 0.143 chance level — under a receptive-field
   projection, against 0.186 as published. Five alternative readouts beside the three
   published ones. The audit's own verdicts survive: every readout of every model stays at
   chance against annotated residues (0.010–0.057, one exception), and none lifts MolTrans
   above the pocket floor.
2. **Masking faithfulness does not transfer across tokenisations (§5b).** Masking
   MolTrans's ten most-attended residues changes **48%** of its ESPF tokens; ten random
   residues change **95%** (for RIPK5, 0.8% against 99%). Its residue-space delta was
   negative in 11 of 12 cells for that reason alone. Measured in token space with both arms
   removing the same number of tokens, the sign reverses in **12 of 12**: MolTrans's
   attention *is* load-bearing. Deltas are comparable within a model across levels, not
   across models (the unit differs).
3. **Attention under-reports what the model uses (§7c).** Integrated gradients on the same
   checkpoints, same ground truth, same protein sets, same test — only the explanation
   differs. **Holm over the IG family: 7 of 12 survive, against 1 of 16 for the attention.**

   | model | attention (UniProt) | gradient | verdict |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | HyperAttentionDTI | 0.034 / 0.040 / 0.025 / 0.022 | **0.055 / 0.079 / 0.079 / 0.060** | survives all four levels, 2.7–4.1× chance |
   | ColdSite-DTI | 0.015 / 0.022 / 0.017 / 0.013 | 0.021 / **0.055** / **0.044** / 0.014 | survives cold-drug + cold-target, 2.3–2.7× |
   | MolTrans | 0.021 / 0.027 / 0.028 / 0.020 | 0.018 / 0.027 / 0.029 / 0.021 | **matches its attention**, at the floor |

   MolTrans is the control that makes this a finding: for two models the attention
   under-reports a site the model *does* represent (a reporting failure); for the third
   there is nothing to report (a model failure). No attention measurement can separate
   those. Computed twice on two accounts with independent T4s — **all 24 cells agree to
   0.0e+00**. IG was added *after* the attention results were seen, so it is a secondary
   analysis, corrected within its own twelve cells, never pooled with the primary sixteen.

### Accuracy, and the benchmark's own faults

* HyperAttentionDTI is the most accurate at every level and the only model that does not
  collapse on unseen drugs: **0.760 ± 0.042** at cold-drug (DeepDTA 0.692, ColdSite-DTI
  0.721, MolTrans 0.685). MolTrans at cold-pair reaches **0.530 ± 0.024 on unseen
  proteins — chance**.
* **DAVIS leaks.** All 54 variant targets with a wild-type entry carry the wild-type
  sequence, so 442 targets are **379 distinct sequences**; 13.6% of cold-target and 12.5%
  of cold-pair test rows are proteins seen in training under another name. Retraining
  DeepDTA on sequence-clean splits with a volume- and class-matched leaky control puts the
  leak at **0.019 of cold-target's 0.038 total drop** — agreeing with the independent
  re-scoring estimate (0.023). Ten targets' sequences lack the kinase pocket.
* **The family confound cannot be tested on these benchmarks.** DAVIS's 6,011 test rows
  contain 3,307 recognised kinase rows and **zero** non-kinase; KIBA is 229 kinases. The
  substitute is transfer to 60 non-kinase BindingDB proteins: both published models are at
  chance there in all eight cells; ColdSite-DTI is above chance in three, traced to a
  **histidine preference** (3–15× enriched) meeting histidine-rich metal sites — the same
  preference that makes it miss the glycine-rich kinase ATP site.
* **Attention does not know which drug binds (§7).** A per-pair ground truth from KLIFS
  interaction fingerprints (217 pairs, 101 proteins, 28 drugs, median 19 contacts) scored
  against the pair's own drug versus **another drug's contacts in the same pocket**: the
  largest gain from the correct drug is **+0.011**, and in two of nine rows the wrong drug
  scores higher. Independently, ColdSite-DTI's drug-blind protein self-attention scores
  *higher* against the pocket than its drug-conditioned cross-attention (0.238 vs 0.219 at
  random; 0.268 vs 0.243 at cold-target).
* **Confidence intervals over proteins (§7d).** Bootstrap resampling proteins, each with
  all its seeds. A 68-protein cell measures precision@10 to ±0.005–0.009 against UniProt
  (narrower than its seed spread) but ±0.020–0.031 against KLIFS. The per-pair drug arms
  run to 0.087 wide, which is why §7 omits cold-pair.

### MolTrans cannot train in float16 — the 2026-09-14 discovery

Both of account C's MolTrans cells reached **NaN loss at batch ~4,040 of epoch 1**, on two
T4s independently, then died 740 validation batches later inside sklearn with
`ValueError: Input contains NaN`.

**Cause, in the vendored model** (`baselines/MolTrans/models.py`): MolTrans defines its own
LayerNorm as `(x − µ) / sqrt(var + 1e-12)`. float16's smallest subnormal is ~6e-8, so
**1e-12 rounds to zero** and a zero-variance row gives 0/0. PyTorch's autocast keeps
`nn.LayerNorm` in fp32 but cannot recognise a hand-written one, and a NaN produced in the
forward pass is in the weights from then on — a GradScaler only rescues *gradient* overflow.

**Why nothing caught it:** the speed test saw 0 non-finite steps in **50 steps at
initialisation**; the pre-flight smoke test ran **one epoch on 80 rows**; and the AMP
validation was **HyperAttentionDTI on one DAVIS cell**, whose result was then applied to all
four models. That last one was the actual mistake and is now written into Limitations as a
reproducibility hazard.

**Fix:** precision is now **per model** — `AMP_BY_MODEL = {'deepdta': True,
'hyperattentiondti': True, 'moltrans': False}`. MolTrans costs 12.4 h/cell instead of 9.4.
Rejected: forcing that LayerNorm to fp32 (which is what autocast does for `nn.LayerNorm`
anyway, so it would not change the mathematics) — it buys 18 GPU-h and ~3 h wall against the
risk of another failed run and an edit to a published baseline the paper must describe
faithfully. fp32 also makes MolTrans's KIBA cells match its DAVIS cells, removing a
comparability caveat.

**Two guards added** so this costs seconds not 17 minutes: MolTrans's training loop stops at
the batch whose loss is non-finite and names the cause and the fix; `compute_metrics` refuses
non-finite predictions before any metric sees them (covers all four trainers). A healthy cell
is unaffected — a test pins that an epoch of finite losses still returns exactly `log(2)` for
a zero-logit model. The one-class-validation NaN (a metric, not a prediction) still returns
NaN rather than raising.

## 7. Working rules (standing)

* **Confirm before any `git push`.**
* Do not change training code while a grid is mid-run; the same applies to
  `src/evaluation/` while `run_all` is running.
* **Ask before starting any long/overnight job on the user's Mac.**
* Never delete the user's data — move to quarantine; the user empties Trash.
* A checkpoint with no `_results.json` beside it is an **interrupted cell, not a result**.
  `run_all` refuses to analyse one. They go to `~/ColdSite-results/partial_do_not_use/`.
* Report split means ± spread over **3 seeds**, never one.
* **Holm–Bonferroni once** across a family fixed in advance.
* Faithfulness is the delta over the random-masking control; the raw score means nothing.
* precision@k always beside chance and ceiling.
* One test pair per protein, one protein per distinct sequence (option A, Methods §2.4).
* The ladder is **not** monotonic on DAVIS — levels are categories, not a severity scale.
* **The data path is not a bottleneck; do not add DataLoader workers.** `num_workers=2` is
  **70× slower** here (0.24 s vs 16.7 s per epoch) because datasets pre-encode in `__init__`.
* Commit messages end `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.
* Explain in plain language; the user prefers step-by-step instructions.

## 8. Traps, and the lessons that cost time

Each of these was found and fixed; they are listed because they are the shape of what goes
wrong here.

* **Verify in the environment where the bug can exist.** An `IG_STEPS` assert importing
  `src` was placed in the settings cell, which runs *before* the repo is cloned. It passed
  locally because the Mac has the repo on `sys.path` — the one environment that cannot catch
  a cell-ordering mistake. A test now runs the settings cell from `/tmp` and asserts no cell
  imports `src` before the clone.
* **A notebook that divides work by split type wastes a GPU.** `kaggle_binary_grid.ipynb`
  uses `SHARE = splits_list[g::N_GPU]`, so an account owning two cells of the same split puts
  both on GPU0. The KIBA notebook schedules per `(model, split, seed)`.
* **Check flags against the real parsers.** The four trainers disagree: HAT and MolTrans have
  no `--task`, `train.py` has no `--patience`, `train_deepdta` has no `--train-subsample`. A
  test greps each generated command against each module's `--help`.
* **Cite the file you actually used.** §5 pointed at a 12-cell audit run with no MolTrans row
  while claiming a 16-cell correction; the claim was right and the pointer was wrong.
* **Delete planning scaffolding when the work lands.** §5 carried a "[Waiting on the analysis
  running 2026-09-14…]" block, with a to-do list, several sections after it was written.
* **A rebuild loses hand-patches.** The KIBA notebook's ETA was applied as a separate patch
  script; re-running the builder silently dropped it. The pipeline is now
  `build_kiba_nb.py` → `add_kiba_eta.py` → `fix_amp_flags.py`, in that order.
* **Small-scale validation does not cover large-scale numerics** — the MolTrans NaN (§4).
* **MolTrans reseeds torch on import** (`torch.manual_seed(1)` in the vendored
  `models.py`), so an early DAVIS run trained one seed three times. Fixed; the runner also
  warns when two seeds of a cell produce identical metrics.
* **MolTrans checkpoints are ~240 MB and its resume file ~754 MB** (HAT 28 MB, ColdSite 7 MB).
  Queue order is most-expensive-first partly so the big model finishes inside commit 1 and
  the resume upload stays small.
